# flat-cats — how we work on this

Read this before touching anything.

The working rules here carry over from the previous project, and they exist for
one reason: **amplification**. Thirteen lines of notes coming back as a 400-line
proposal and eight commits is not productivity — no human can review at that
ratio. The point is not slower work; it is work Zane can still see into on day
thirty.

## 1. One change at a time

- One commit = one change = one sentence. If the commit subject needs a comma
  splice or a list of IDs, it is more than one change.
- **Never** land several unrelated items in one go because they were all in the
  same note. Six notes means six changes, reviewed six times, in whatever order
  Zane wants them.
- Do not open a second front while one is unreviewed.

## 2. Ask before building, not after

For anything that is a **judgement call** — which images belong in the set, what
counts as a good matte, whether a part label earns its place, what the prompts
should say — post the intent first, in five lines or fewer:

```
What I'd change:  drop the 'leg' prompt, fold it into 'torso'
What you'd see:   fewer speckled leg fragments on lying-down cats
Where:            segment.py PARTS, PROMPTS
Risk:             standing cats lose a real distinction
```

Then stop. Cheap to read, cheap to reject.

For anything **mechanical** — a bug, a crash, a rename, a measurement — just do
it and say what you did.

## 3. Every change ships with something to look at

A diff is not a review artifact for an image pipeline. Before saying a change is
done, produce the thing that lets Zane judge it with his eyes:

```sh
.venv/bin/python segment.py --only cat_01 cat_14 cat_21 cat_34
.venv/bin/python sheet.py out/overlay out/_sheet.png --cols 6
```

Keep the same few cats as the standing comparison set. If a change is not better
side by side, it is not better.

## 4. Size budget — the tripwire

Soft ceilings. These are not style rules; they are the point at which a file
stops being readable in one sitting:

| File | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| any script at the root | 400 lines |
| any single function | 60 lines |

**When a change would break a ceiling, stop and say so before writing it.** The
answer might be "fine, go ahead" — but it has to be Zane's answer.

Also stop and check in if a single turn is about to produce more than ~150 lines
of new code that hasn't been discussed.

## 5. Things that must stay true

- **`images/` is CC0 or public domain, and that is checked twice.** The Commons
  search filter is never the only thing standing between the dataset and a
  share-alike image; `download.py` re-checks each candidate's own licence
  metadata against `ALLOWED`. Do not relax either check. Anything dropped is
  dropped loudly.
- **`CREDITS.md` and `images/credits.json` stay in step with `images/`.** They
  are generated by `download.py`; regenerate, don't hand-edit.
- **`out/` is derived and disposable.** Nothing may read from `out/` as if it
  were input, and nothing in it gets committed.
- **`segment.py` stage 2 is clipped to stage 1's matte.** No part label may
  exist outside the foreground.

## 6. No parallel agents, no worktrees

One session, one thread, one change. Do not spawn subagents to parallelise work
here.

## 7. Reporting

Say what actually happened. If something fails, show it. If you skipped part of a
request, name the part. If you're unsure whether a change improved the output,
say that instead of asserting it did — you cannot see it, and Zane can.
